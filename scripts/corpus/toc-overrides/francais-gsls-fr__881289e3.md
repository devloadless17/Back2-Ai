The GS/LS French anthology. Its contents table is laid out in columns — theme,
sub-theme, the descriptive label of the sub-theme, the source text, the author,
and a column of page numbers down the side — and extraction interleaves all of
them. The automatic parse produced eleven "chapters" whose numbers are page
numbers (13, 15, 19, 22, 26...) and whose names are the anthology's furniture:

  Sous-thème 1              17-17
  Exercices d'entraînement  17-23
  Sous-thème 2              23-24
  Production écrite         26-28    and again at 43-147
  Lexique notionnel         28-32

Every theme in the book contains a "Sous-thème 1", a "Sous-thème 2", an
"Exercices d'entraînement", a "Production écrite" and a "Lexique notionnel". As
chapter names those are not wrong so much as useless: six chapters share each
name, a student sees five copies of "Production écrite" in their syllabus, and
the forty-four past-exam questions filed across them give no way to tell which
theme any of them belongs to.

(Numerals are spelled out in this comment on purpose. A line here beginning
with digits is read as a chapter by the same bare-numbered pattern the entries
below rely on, and an earlier draft of this note produced a thirteenth chapter
called "44 past-exam questions are filed across the".)

The real chapters are the twelve SUB-THEMES, and their real names are the
descriptive labels the contents page prints beside each one — "La bioéthique",
"Personnage comique" — not the position. Those are what a student would
recognise and what an exam question is about.

Page numbers are left out. They are printed in a detached column and cannot be
paired with entries with any confidence — the THEME 3 block alone gives 46, 47,
48, 53, 60, 62, 63 against five entries — and a wrong printed page poisons the
offset vote for the whole book.

The back matter is not chapters and is left out: LE TEXTE DRAMATIQUE,
LITTERATURE DU XXe SIECLE, FICHES TECHNIQUES and the three discourse annexes
are reference material the book appends after the six themes.
