## Fourth exercise: (7 points) — Nuclear reactions

Given: mass of a proton: $m_p = 1.0073\ \mathrm{u}$; mass of a neutron: $m_n = 1.0087\ \mathrm{u}$;

mass of $^{235}_{92}\mathrm{U}$ nucleus $= 235.0439\ \mathrm{u}$; mass of $^{90}_{36}\mathrm{Kr}$ nucleus $= 89.9197\ \mathrm{u}$;

mass of $^{142}_{Z}\mathrm{Ba}$ nucleus $= 141.9164\ \mathrm{u}$; molar mass of $^{235}_{92}\mathrm{U} = 235\ \mathrm{g/mole}$;

Avogadro's number: $N_A = 6.022\times10^{23}\ \mathrm{mol^{-1}}$; $1\,\mathrm{u} = 931.5\ \mathrm{MeV/c^2} = 1.66\times10^{-27}\ \mathrm{kg}$; $1\ \mathrm{MeV} = 1.6\times10^{-13}\ \mathrm{J}$.

### A – Provoked nuclear reaction

As a result of collision with a thermal neutron, a uranium 235 nucleus undergoes the following reaction:

$$^{1}_{0}\mathrm{n} + ^{235}_{92}\mathrm{U} \longrightarrow ^{90}_{36}\mathrm{Kr} + ^{142}_{Z}\mathrm{Ba} + y\,^{1}_{0}\mathrm{n}$$

**1) a)** Determine $y$ and $z$.
&nbsp;&nbsp;**b)** Indicate the type of this provoked nuclear reaction.

**2)** Calculate, in MeV, the energy liberated by this reaction.

**3)** In fact, 7% of this energy appears as a kinetic energy of all the produced neutrons.
&nbsp;&nbsp;**a)** Determine the speed of each neutron knowing that they have equal kinetic energy.
&nbsp;&nbsp;**b)** A thermal neutron, that can provoke nuclear fission, must have a speed of few km/s; indicate then the role of the "moderator" in a nuclear reactor.

**4)** In a nuclear reactor with uranium 235, the average energy liberated by the fission of one nucleus is 170 MeV.
&nbsp;&nbsp;**a)** Determine, in joules, the average energy liberated by the fission of one kg of uranium $^{235}_{92}\mathrm{U}$.
&nbsp;&nbsp;**b)** The nuclear power of such reactor is 100 MW. Calculate the time $\Delta t$ needed so that the reactor consumes one kg of uranium $^{235}_{92}\mathrm{U}$.

### B – Spontaneous nuclear reaction

**1)** The nucleus krypton $^{90}_{36}\mathrm{Kr}$ obtained is radioactive. It disintegrates into zirconium $^{90}_{40}\mathrm{Zr}$, by a series of $\beta^{-}$ disintegrations.
&nbsp;&nbsp;**a)** Determine the number of $\beta^{-}$ disintegrations.
&nbsp;&nbsp;**b)** Specify, without calculation, which one of the two nuclides $^{90}_{36}\mathrm{Kr}$ and $^{90}_{40}\mathrm{Zr}$ is more stable.

**2)** Uranium $^{235}_{92}\mathrm{U}$ is an $\alpha$ emitter.
&nbsp;&nbsp;**a)** Write down the equation of disintegration of uranium $^{235}_{92}\mathrm{U}$ and identify the nucleus produced.

&nbsp;&nbsp;Given: Actinium $_{89}\mathrm{Ac}$ | Thorium $_{90}\mathrm{Th}$ | Protactinium $_{91}\mathrm{Pa}$

&nbsp;&nbsp;**b)** The remaining number of nuclei of $^{235}_{92}\mathrm{U}$ as a function of time is given by: $N = N_0\,e^{-\lambda t}$ where $N_0$ is the number of the nuclei of $^{235}_{92}\mathrm{U}$ at $t_0 = 0$ and $\lambda$ is the decay constant of $^{235}_{92}\mathrm{U}$.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**i)** Define the activity $A$ of a radioactive sample.
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**ii)** Write the expression of $A$ in terms of $\lambda$, $N_0$ and time $t$.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**c)** Derive the expression of $\ln(A)$ in terms of the initial activity $A_0$, $\lambda$ and $t$.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**d)** The adjacent figure represents the variation of $\ln(A)$ of a sample of $^{235}_{92}\mathrm{U}$ as a function of time.
